When You Turn Seventeen

Date: Nov 23, 2026

Julian,

When you turn seventeen, I want you to ask me what I think you’re old enough to do on your own. Let me finish answering. Then remind me how old I was when I left Albania.

I suspect you’ll enjoy that conversation.

I was seventeen. I had finished my junior year of high school in Tirana and came to America on my own. You’ll hear me tell that story over the years. What interests me now, as your father, is how much of it I still understand from only one side.

At seventeen, I knew what it felt like to leave. I had very little idea what it felt like to be my father and watch me go. Even now I only have the beginnings of an idea. You’re still young enough to want me to sit with you until you fall asleep. Ten years sounds like a long time until I realize that’s roughly how long I have before you’re the age I was.

Your grandfather lives in the same house as us. One day, ask him what he remembers about that time. Ask your grandmother too. Give them room to tell the parts I would leave out simply because I wasn’t there for them. I came to America. They went home that evening. I wonder what they talked about.

There’s something else I want to settle while you’re young. I know how easily a father’s story can become a measuring stick in his son’s hands. If you’re seventeen and happy where you are, stay. If something worth doing takes you far from me, tell me about it. I’ll probably ask too many questions. Give me a little time. I’ll be learning my side of that conversation too.

And if I ever start a sentence with “When I was your age,” you have my permission to say, “Babo, let me tell you what it’s like to be my age now.”

I’d like to hear that part.

Love you more always,
Babo
