Go Back Upstairs

Date: Nov 23, 2026

Julian,

Suppose I send you upstairs because I’m sure you lost something I told you to put away. You tell me you never touched it. I give you a whole speech about taking responsibility. Ten minutes later I open a drawer and find the thing exactly where I left it.

Now we get to see how much your father knows about taking responsibility.

I could call up the stairs, “I found it. You can come down.” We could eat dinner. By bedtime you might even forget about it. And if I wanted to feel a little better about myself, I could remember the last three things you actually did leave lying around. Funny how quickly I can come up with a case against you once the one I had falls apart.

Meanwhile, you’re upstairs. You told your father the truth. He looked you in the eye and decided you were lying. Finding the thing in the drawer settles my problem. You still have yours.

I owe you the walk upstairs. I owe you the words. “Julian, I found it where I put it. You told me the truth, and I didn’t believe you. I was wrong to send you up here. I’m sorry.”

Then I owe you enough time to tell me what that felt like, even if dinner’s ready and I’d much rather be done.

One day you’ll be on my side of that door. Maybe it’ll be with your sister. You’ll say something unfair to Leila in front of other people, and later, when the two of you are alone, she’ll let you apologize quietly. Think about who heard you the first time. If you made her look dishonest in front of the family, the family ought to hear you say she told the truth. That part is yours to straighten out. She shouldn’t have to ask.

The older you get, the fewer people can make you do this. A six-year-old can’t order his father back upstairs. That’s exactly why it matters that I go.

Love you more always,
Babo
