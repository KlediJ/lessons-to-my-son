Strength and Honor

Date: Nov 23, 2026

Julian,

One time, while I was competing with Idi, I told you winning wasn’t everything. You said, “Yes, that’s right, Babo.” And then I kept talking, because I’d only told you half of what I believe. Winning is very important. I always want you to want to win. What matters most to me is that you do it with strength and honor.

I know how much you hate losing. Sometimes you’d rather sit out a game you want to play because you know how you’ll feel if you lose. I understand that feeling. You’ve seen me compete with Idi. Believe me, I want to beat him. But think about all the good times I’d have missed with him if I only played on days I was sure I’d win.

That’s where strength comes in, son. Sometimes it takes everything you have just to say, “I’ll play.” Nobody watching knows what that cost you. I know. The other kid gets a say in who wins. Whether you play is yours to decide. Even if losing bothers you all the way home, you still did the thing you were afraid to do. You can do it again.

When you’re older, there’s a moment in Gladiator I’d like you to watch with me. Maximus has won. The emperor orders him to kill the man he’s beaten, and Maximus lets him live. The crowd cheers him for it. What I want you to notice is how late that cheer comes. He’s already made his choice. He had to be willing to hear them boo.

That’s what I mean by honor. When I compete with Idi, I want an honest answer to a simple question. Can I beat him today? If I have to cheat him, even in some small way he’ll never know about, I’ve spoiled the very thing I wanted to find out. And if he beats me fair and square, he deserves to enjoy beating me. God knows I want to enjoy it when I beat him.

One day you might lose and look over at me, wondering what I think. I’ll tell you now. I wanted you to win too. Come sit next to me. You can tell me about the part you wish you could play over. I’ll listen.

And the next time they ask if you want to play, I hope you say yes.

Love you more always,
Babo
